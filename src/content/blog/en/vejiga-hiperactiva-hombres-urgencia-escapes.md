# Overactive Bladder in Men: Urgency, Leaks and Treatment

Ramiro works at a warehouse in the East End. A few months ago he started noticing something he kept to himself: out of nowhere, the urge to pee hits so hard that he has to drop whatever he is carrying and rush off. Twice he did not make it. Since then he keeps a spare T-shirt in the truck.

If that sounds familiar, you are not alone. Urgency and leaks are more common in men than most people think, yet hardly anyone talks about them. Here we explain what this no-warning urge is, how it differs from other leaks and what you can do at home while the cause is looked into.

## A signal that shows up late and in a hurry

The bladder is a muscle that fills slowly and normally sends a gentle signal around halfway, while you can still wait. With an overactive bladder, that muscle squeezes too early, like someone pressing on a half-inflated balloon. The result is a sudden urge that is hard to hold, and sometimes a leak before you reach the toilet.

Not every leak is the same, and knowing yours helps at the visit:

| What you notice | Usual name | Main clue |
|---|---|---|
| A strong urge out of nowhere, and urine escapes before you get there | Urge leakage | No strain beforehand |
| Urine escapes when you cough, lift or stand up from a chair | Stress leakage | Triggered by belly pressure |
| A few drops after you zip up | Post-void dribble | Seconds after you finish |
| Near-constant dribbling and a feeling of never emptying | Possible retention | Needs to be checked soon |

You can have more than one type at once. Overactive bladder is mostly the first row: urgency is the core symptom, with or without a leak.

## Why does it happen to men?

Often there is no single cause. Among those the medical team at Clínica Hispana Nueva Salud Lawndale usually considers:

- **The prostate.** When it grows and squeezes the urine outlet, the bladder works overtime and becomes irritable.
- **A urinary infection**, which can mimic an overactive bladder within a few days. That is why ruling it out usually comes first.
- **High blood sugar.** Poorly controlled diabetes increases the amount of urine and, over the years, can affect the bladder's nerves.
- **Constipation**, because a full bowel presses on the bladder from behind.
- **Some medicines and nerve problems**, such as the aftermath of a stroke or a back injury.

## Three days with pen and paper: the bladder diary

Before changing anything, measure. For three days in a row, jot down in a notebook or on your phone:

1. When you urinated, each time, with the clock time.
2. Whether you felt urgency, from 0 to 3 (3 means "I barely made it").
3. Whether there was a leak and what you were doing.
4. What you drank and roughly how much.

By day three a pattern usually appears: urges cluster mid-morning, or after the soda at lunch, or every time you reach your front door and pull out your keys. That last one is common: the brain links certain moments with urinating and fires the urge out of habit. Bring the diary to your visit; it beats any memory.

## Retraining the bladder so it can hold again

The goal is for the muscle to fill up again without complaining:

1. **Check your diary** and work out how often, on average, you go during the day.
2. **Set a schedule** a little longer than that. If you go every hour, start with every hour and fifteen minutes, even without the need.
3. **If the urge comes early**, do not run. Stay still, squeeze your pelvic floor three or four times quickly, breathe slowly and think about something else. The wave usually fades within a minute or two; then walk calmly to the bathroom if you still need to.
4. **Stretch the interval** by about fifteen minutes each week, until you reach three or four hours.

Rushing while the bladder is contracting makes leaks worse, because the sudden movement raises the pressure.

## The pelvic floor: men have one too

These are the muscles that support the bladder and help close off the urine outlet.

**How to find it.** Imagine holding in gas in public. When you squeeze correctly, the base of the penis pulls up a little and the testicles lift slightly. Stopping your stream midway is only for finding the muscle once; do not use it as an exercise, because it can leave urine behind.

**The basic routine:**

- Squeeze for three to five seconds, then relax for the same time.
- Ten repetitions, three times a day: lying down at first, then sitting and, once you have it down, standing.
- Breathe normally. If you hold your breath, clench your buttocks or pull in your belly, you are using the wrong muscle.

**Two tricks.** Squeeze right before you cough, sneeze or lift something heavy. And for the after-dribble, a firm squeeze when you finish, or gentle pressure behind the scrotum moving forward, clears the last drops before you get dressed.

You will not notice a change in the first week; most men see it after six to twelve weeks of daily practice.

## Habits that give your bladder a hand

- **Do not cut back on water out of fear of leaking.** Very concentrated urine irritates the bladder; spread your fluids over the day instead.
- **Look after your bowels** with fiber, fruit and vegetables.
- **If you smoke, work on quitting:** a chronic cough pushes urine out over and over.
- **Skip the "just in case" trip** every time you leave the house; that habit teaches the bladder to ask with little urine inside.

## When not to wait: call 911

Urgency is a nuisance, but it is rarely dangerous. These situations are, and they should not wait for an appointment:

- You have gone hours unable to pass urine, and your lower belly is hard and painful.
- A high fever with chills that make you shake comes together with burning when you urinate.
- Your urine turns red or has clots and you feel weak or close to fainting.
- You suddenly lose bladder control while your legs feel weak or the area between your thighs goes numb.
- One testicle starts hurting suddenly and very badly.

If any of these happens, dial 911 or head to an ER right away instead of stopping by the clinic first.
